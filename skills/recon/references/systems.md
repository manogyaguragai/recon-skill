# Funding systems

Country models are not variations on a theme. They are different games, and the
same profile is strong in one and weak in another. Getting this wrong is the
most common reason a capable applicant gets nothing.

**This file describes structures, not current facts.** Specific figures,
deadlines, and eligibility rules go stale. Always verify against the primary
source at run time - see `evidence.md`.

## The four models

**Employment model** - Germany, Netherlands, Nordics, and much of northern
Europe. The doctorate is a salaried research post inside a group, not a
coursework programme. The supervisor holds a project budget and hires. There
may be no "programme" to apply to at all. Consequences: direct supervisor
contact is the main route and is expected, not presumptuous; positions appear
as job vacancies; funding is taxed and gross is not net; social contributions
and pension deductions can move a headline stipend substantially. An applicant
carrying their own portable scholarship is dramatically more attractive here,
because they remove the budget constraint entirely.

**Programme-admission model** - United States, Canada, and similar. Apply to a
department; funding arrives bundled as an assistantship or fellowship after
admission. Faculty influence varies enormously and so does their appetite for
being contacted - some actively want early contact, others publish explicit
do-not-email policies and treat prior contact as irrelevant or negative. Check
each individual's stated position before writing. Blanket "email professors"
advice is wrong here often enough to be dangerous.

**Central-scholarship model** - MEXT, GKS, Chinese Government Scholarship,
Türkiye Bursları and similar. A government body funds; the university is
downstream. Frequently multiple tracks with *different requirements for the
same award* - an embassy route and a university route, with different
competitiveness, different documents, and sometimes a rule against applying to
both. Track selection is a real strategic decision and must be surfaced as one.
Hard eligibility bars are common and unforgiving: age limits, total-years-of-
education minimums, nationality restrictions, bars on holding other government
funding, waiting periods for former recipients, no deferrals.

**Structured doctoral programmes** - research training groups, doctoral
training partnerships and centres, Marie Sklodowska-Curie doctoral networks,
graduate schools attached to research institutes. Annual intakes, cohort based,
often with a fixed set of pre-defined projects. These generate no news between
cycles and are the class that freshness-based searching misses entirely. Look
for the current call rather than recent announcements.

## What varies and must be checked per target

- **Whether contacting a supervisor helps, is required, or hurts.** In the
  central-scholarship model it is sometimes formally required to have an
  acceptance letter before applying. In the programme-admission model it can be
  irrelevant. Never assume.
- **Total years of education counting.** Systems that require sixteen years
  exclude three-year bachelor holders outright, no matter how strong. This is
  the single most common invisible disqualifier and it must be checked first.
  Coursework and contact-hour evidence sometimes supports an equivalence case.
- **Degree recency.** Some schemes bar applicants whose last degree is older
  than a set number of years.
- **Residency and where the last degree was earned.** Some schemes require the
  applicant to have been resident, or to have studied, in their home country.
- **Country quotas.** Where awards are allocated per country, competitiveness
  depends on the applicant's passport rather than their CV. A strong candidate
  from a small-quota country faces far worse odds than the same candidate
  elsewhere. Allocations are frequently unpublished - say so rather than
  guessing.
- **Employment versus scholarship status.** Determines tax, social security,
  work rights, family rights, and whether the years count toward permanent
  residence.
- **Post-study pathway.** Job-seeker visas, points-system recognition, and
  routes to residency are the concrete component of "doors opened" and are
  documentable, unlike prestige.

## Under-searched routes

Most applicants look at the same twenty famous scholarships. The odds are
better in the places nobody looks.

- Industrial and employer-funded doctorates
- Bilateral agreements between the user's country and a specific partner
- Institute-based routes outside universities - national labs, research
  societies, non-university research institutes
- Field-specific foundations and learned societies
- Regional and sub-national awards
- Small-quota schemes where the applicant pool is tiny

Discovery is uncapped for exactly this reason. Niche is where the odds live.
